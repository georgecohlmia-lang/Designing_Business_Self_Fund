STEP THREE · THE DIAGNOSTIC

Three questions. Answer honestly.

Two or three "no" answers means you're building a business that needs outside capital — which is a legitimate choice, but it should be a deliberate one, not a surprise in month six.

Question 01 · Margin
Does each sale make enough money — after the cost of delivering it — to actually matter?
Does each member contribute enough each month — after serving them — to actually matter?

A $100 sale at 20% margin contributes $20. To cover $5,000 of fixed cost you'd need 250 sales. If 250 isn't realistic, the answer is no.

A $50 membership at 70% margin contributes $35. To cover $5,000 of fixed cost you'd need 143 active members. If that's not realistic, the answer is no.

Question 02 · Timing
Does cash arrive in time to fund the next round of costs?
Is most of your monthly revenue reliable — autopay or contracts that just renew?

If you're spending in week 1 and collecting in week 12 with payroll every two weeks, the answer is probably no — even if margins look fine.

If 90%+ of members are on autopay or annual contracts that auto-renew, yes. If you have to chase invoices each month, no.

Question 03 · Volume
Is there a realistic path to the sales volume that covers your fixed costs?
Is there a realistic path to the active member count that covers your fixed costs?

"Realistic" means with your current capacity, marketing channels, and pipeline. Not "if everything goes great."

"Realistic" means net of churn, with your current acquisition channels and capacity. Not "if everything goes great."

Please answer all three questions before generating your strategy.
