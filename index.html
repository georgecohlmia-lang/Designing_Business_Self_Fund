STEP THREE · THE LEVERS

Three levers close the gap.

A cash-timing gap is not a fact you accept — it is something you design around. For each lever, write the one specific move you can make in your business. Start with the first; it is the one owners resist most and almost always the fastest win.

LEVER 01 Accelerate collection

Move customers toward faster payment, or offer a small early-payment discount. Most owners never run the math on what the timing improvement is worth — run it before you decide a customer would say no.

LEVER 02 Delay cost until revenue is confirmed

Some costs are fixed — limited room there. But others can move from "paid up front" to "paid once the sale is real." Christine's example: structuring manufacturer orders so product is produced against confirmed orders, not carried on faith — often one conversation with a supplier.

LEVER 03 Increase margin per transaction

Not necessarily by raising price. Channel mix moves the blended margin on its own — a direct channel carries a different margin than a wholesale or referral one, and as the mix shifts, the self-funding math gets easier without touching price at all.

THE DIAGNOSTIC

Can your model actually get there?

Three questions. All three yes, and you have a self-funding business. One no is usually a fixable structural problem. Two or three no, and you are building a business that needs outside capital — a legitimate choice, but it should be deliberate, not a surprise in month six.

Question 01 · Margin
Does your margin generate enough cash per sale to actually matter?

A sale's margin contribution has to make a real dent in fixed cost. If the break-even count above is far beyond what you can realistically reach, the answer is no.

Question 02 · Timing
Are your payment terms short enough that cash arrives before the next cost cycle?

Compare your total cash cycle from Step 2 against how often your fixed costs fall — payroll every two weeks, rent monthly. If you spend in week one and collect in week twelve, the answer is probably no, even if margins look fine.

Question 03 · Volume
Is there a realistic path to the volume that covers your fixed costs before reserves run out?

"Realistic" means with your current capacity, channels, and pipeline — not "if everything goes great." For recurring revenue, realistic means net of churn.

THE VERDICT
Design it — don't discover it. Know your break-even floor before you make your next hiring or investment decision. Make the next hire only after the current structure has proven it can fund itself.
